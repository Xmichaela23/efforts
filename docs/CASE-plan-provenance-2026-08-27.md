# THE CASE FOR THIS PLAN — every claim, and the page behind it

**What this is.** Strong Focus is assembled, not copied out of the book. This is the defence of that
assembly: what came off a page, which page, and the short list of things we decided ourselves.
Written 2026-08-27 against `SOURCE-viada-hybrid-athlete.md`, which is the real record and outranks
this summary.

**Written in plain English on purpose.** The only shorthand used is Viada's own — ME, DE, SKILL, HYP
for the four lifting intents, and MLSS, NT, VT1, LSD for the four running session types. Both sets
are his and appear on his pages. None of our internal words appear here.

⛔ **When any line here matters, open the page.** The photographs are at
`~/Efforts_Local_Folder/book-sources/viada-hybrid-athlete/`, on this machine only.

## 1. THE ATHLETE THIS IS FOR IS IN THE BOOK — twice, and with a number once

This plan serves someone who runs 10 to 30 miles a week, rides at weekends, and would never call
themselves a hybrid athlete. Viada has no weekly plan written for that person. He does describe them.

- **p137, with a number.** *"If you're a hybrid runner and have determined that 18 to 20 miles per
  week running at various intensities represents your current upper bound, adding two hours of
  cycling or Arc Trainer/elliptical per week will almost certainly aid your running, as long as you
  properly maintain recovery."*
- **p85, the same person from the other side.** The cyclist who also wants muscle, and why the
  weight-room work has to be *"far more conservative"* when leg days eat the riding days.
- **p247, who this program is for.** *"…accessible and useful for athletes of most skill levels. The
  lifting component is perfectly acceptable for competitive powerlifters, while the running program
  can be of use for even advanced intermediate runners."*

⛔ **So the athlete is sourced. What is missing is a week written for them** — and filling that in is
the entire job this plan does. Section 8 is what it cost.

## 4. THE BIKE — the biggest call in the plan, and the pages that carry it

Strength + 5K prescribes every endurance session as a run. Putting a bike in it is the largest single
decision here. It is allowed, in this order:

1. **Pages 135 to 138 — his cross-training rules.** Get proficient at the new sport first;
   **cross-train the easy volume, never the hard work**; hard work in another sport **only when your
   running volume is already capped.**
2. **p137 — the prescription with a number in it.** Two hours of cycling for a runner at their
   mileage ceiling *"will almost certainly aid your running."* Same page states the rule plainly:
   *"when in doubt, use cross-training for easy work, not threshold or sprint work."*
3. **p138 — the exception that allows a HARD ride.** *"Consider adding some work at higher intensity
   with similar modalities IF you're really pushing the limits of your tolerable volume and you can't
   otherwise figure out how to break through to the next level."*
4. **p247 — the program's own permission.** Bigger athletes who take more wear from running *"may
   want to convert the Wednesday session into a cross-training VT1 session."*
5. **p275 — swapping sports, and the long ride.** Allows the swap outright, and says a long ride can
   stand in for the long run.

⛔ **And the ride you get is the gentler option, by design.** Every session type in the book states
how hard its work is, as a percentage of your threshold. We matched each ride to the run it replaces
on that number, so the ride is never harder than the run it stands in for:

    hard steady run   works at 100%   →  sweet spot ride, medium   works at 95–100%
    near-threshold run works at 85%   →  sweet spot ride, long     works at 90%
    easy or long run  no floor        →  endurance ride            below 75%

⚠️ **The VO2 ride was the trap, and it is explicitly refused.** Its work sits at 110% — harder than
the run it would have replaced. Choosing it would have quietly made your week harder while calling
itself a swap.

⛔ **A hard ride can never go above level 2**, wherever the level came from. His own cycling programs
(p278, p280) never go higher. And the sweet spot ride's stated purpose, pp.238–239, is *"as close to
threshold as possible without exceeding it"* — which is exactly what the run it replaces is for.

## 6. THE LIFTING IS ENTIRELY HIS — Wendler is deliberately out of this plan

⛔ **There is no Wendler in Strong Focus, and keeping it out is a ruling.** *"If wendler has a future
at all its not in this path"* (Michael, 2026-08-27).

**The sets and reps are p218's table, exactly as printed:**

| intent | reps | load | reps left in the tank | sets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ME — heavy | 1–5 | 90–100% | none stated | 1–3 |
| DE — fast | 2–4 | 70–80% | 3–4 | 4–6 |
| SKILL — practice | 3–5 | 75–85% | 3–4 | 3–5 |
| HYP — size | 6–12 | — | 0–2 | 3–4 |

**p219 says what each one is for**, including the rule that a heavy set stops short of failure
because form breaking down there works against you.

⛔ **Every weight in the block is priced off one number, and it comes from p215.** Your last test set
goes through two standard one-rep-max estimates, the two are averaged, and the number the plan spends
is **96% of that**.

⛔⛔ **That is NOT the same as Wendler's number, and the two are kept apart on purpose.**

    Wendler   85% of a true one-rep max        Get Stronger only
    Viada     96% of a freshly predicted one   this plan, and nothing else

They never convert into each other and nothing in the code accepts both, because that would be the
door one leaks through. Wendler's own machinery is untouched and Get Stronger still runs on it.

⚠️ **THE SOURCE RECORD CONTAINS AN OLD RECOMMENDATION THAT WAS NEVER TAKEN.** Part F of
`SOURCE-viada-hybrid-athlete.md`, written 21 August, suggests running Wendler in the heavy slot and
lists the three problems that would create. **It was never adopted.** It predates this plan and is
history, not what ships. This file's first draft read it as fact and was corrected the same day.

## 8. ⛔ WHAT IS NOT ON A PAGE — the whole list

Three things, and they are all joins between his parts rather than numbers of our own.

1. **Which ride replaces which run.** p275 allows the swap and never says which ride stands in for
   which run, and nothing else in the book does either. The RULE we matched them on is his — every
   session states how hard its work is — but the pairing table is ours.
2. **Where in his range we build a hard session.** He gives a range; we build one fixed point inside
   it and the session never moves off it. ⚠️ **What was killed was different and should not be
   confused with this**: the athlete's weekly hours used to stretch the hard session, which had it
   backwards, and that is gone. The session is now fixed. Choosing WHERE to fix it is still ours.
3. **Which repeat length goes with which repeat count.** He prints specific pairs — four 5-minute
   efforts, four 8-minute, three 12-minute, three 15-minute. Between those pairs we build inside his
   ranges.

⛔⛔ **NUMBER 3 IS LIVE IN A SHIPPED PLAN AND IT IS THE ONE AN EXPERT WOULD CATCH.** The Wednesday
near-threshold run at level 3 builds as **five efforts of 8:30** at 85–92%, with 1:15 of easy jogging
between. **p234 prints that session as FOUR rounds of 8:30 + 1 minute at VT1.** Five sits inside his
level 3 work range and is printed nowhere in the book.

## ⛔ WHY 1, 2 AND 3 EXIST — one decision, showing up three times

**The library was built from his METHOD, not from his printed workouts.** The file's own header says
so: *"THIS FILE HOLDS RULES, NOT HIS SESSION TABLES. His interval tables and movement lists are his
EXPRESSION. What is here is method — intensity bands, rep-length bands, work:rest, floors, caps."*

Two reasons, and both still hold:

- **His printed sessions are his book.** Copying his workout tables into an app is reproducing the
  book. Holding his method is not.
- **His sessions are written generically.** Ours are built against the athlete's own threshold and
  rotated week to week, which a fixed list of his workouts cannot do.

⛔ **So numbers 2 and 3 are the same decision twice.** Holding a range means something must choose a
point in it (2), and the repeat count then falls out of arithmetic on the range rather than being read
off his printed pair (3). **Five where he printed four is the cost of not reproducing his book, not a
mistake.** The answer to an expert is *"we did not copy his sessions, on purpose"* — not *"we got it
wrong."*

⚠️ **WORTH TIGHTENING, NOT YET DONE.** Where our repeat length lands exactly on one of his printed
pairs — 8:30 is literally his number — we could take his rep count instead of computing one. His own
printed sessions would then come out exactly, and the arithmetic would only fill the gaps between
them. Raised 2026-08-27, not ruled on.

## 8a. ✅ CLOSED — the hard ride's condition IS met, structurally

**Raised and closed 2026-08-27.** Pages 135 to 138 make hard work in another sport conditional —
*"only when volume-capped"*, and *"when in doubt"* cross-train the easy work — and the setup screen
appears to offer a hard ride as a free choice. It is not free.

⛔ **The plan holds endurance flat for all twelve weeks, at the hours the athlete said they can hold
comfortably. It never grows their running.** So the running volume IS capped, by the design of the
program rather than by a check at the point of choosing. That is p138's condition, met by
construction (Michael, 2026-08-27).

⚠️ **This closes only while that stays true.** A future change that lets the plan grow an athlete's
running would re-open it, because then the hard ride would be offered to someone whose running is not
at its ceiling.

## 8b. DEPARTURES — where we read the page and went the other way

Not gaps. Decisions made with the page in front of us.

- **p247 offers stronger runners *"one or two VT1 sessions"* of extra volume.** We decline it. The
  rule is convert, never add — the program owns how many sessions a week holds. His own optional
  extra, deliberately not taken.

## 9. WHAT THE ATHLETE IS TOLD

The honest split, and the copy has to stay on the right side of it:

- **HIS** — the difficulty levels, the effort ranges, the shape of each session, the warm-up and
  cool-down, and the session lengths those produce.
- **OURS** — the exact combination of repeat length and repeat count inside them.

Telling someone their hard run is 66 minutes is a claim about what that choice gives them, and it is
true. Telling them it is *"your session from p234"* would not be. ⛔ **Never put his name on a
specific workout.**
