# REFERENCE — exercise substitution and option ranking

**What this is.** The evidence behind two decisions the app makes constantly and had been making on
judgement: *is this substitute like-for-like?* and *in what order do a cell's options appear?*

**Read it like `SOURCE-viada-hybrid-athlete.md`:** every claim carries its source. Where the field
genuinely disagrees, or is silent, this file says so rather than papering over it. ⛔ **Nothing here
is an invented principle.** A rule with no citation is marked OURS and says what it was derived from.

⚠️ **Provenance is not uniform and the sections say which they are.** §1–§3 rest on primary research
and NSCA-grade material. §4 rests on secondary sources — review sites describing what apps do, not
the apps' own documentation — and is labelled accordingly. Do not cite §4 as if it were §1.

## §1. What makes a substitution like-for-like

### 1a. Modality is not the thing that matters — the muscle and the pattern are

A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis (13 studies, 1,016 participants) compared free-weight and
machine-based training directly. **No differences were detected between modalities** for dynamic
strength, isometric strength, hypertrophy or countermovement jump. The authors' conclusion: *"the
choice between free-weights and machines are down to individual preferences and goals."*
· [Free-weight vs machine meta-analysis, PMC10426227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)

⛔ **THE CAVEAT IS THE LOAD-BEARING HALF, and it is the authors' own emphasis:** *"strength changes
are specific to the training modality."* Tested in the modality trained, each group won its own test.
So swapping a machine for a free-weight version is sound **for hypertrophy and for general strength**,
and is NOT sound when the tested outcome is the machine movement itself.

**What this licenses in the app:** substituting a stack for a dumbbell in an accessory/hypertrophy
cell. **What it forbids:** doing the same to a movement the block MEASURES from — the competition
lifts the test week reads.

### 1b. Stability differs even when the muscle does not

Free-weight execution requires the lifter to balance and stabilise the implement; machine execution
removes that demand. NSCA/Human Kinetics material reports **back stabiliser activity roughly 30%
lower in a Smith-machine squat than in a free-weight squat**, and notes machines' compensating
advantage: stability *"can target specific muscle groups better."*
· [NSCA — types of resistance training equipment](https://www.nsca.com/certification/nsca-cpt/essentials-of-personal-training--3rd-edition/excerpts/types-of-resistance-training-equipment/)
· [Human Kinetics — same excerpt](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/types-of-resistance-training-equipment)

**What this licenses:** treating stability as a real axis of difference, not a tie-breaker to ignore.
A swap that changes stability demand is a swap the athlete should be able to see.

### 1c. Resistance profile and muscle length are part of "same movement"

Where an exercise is hard through its range is a property of the exercise, not of the muscle. Stronger
by Science: resistance profiles *"show how tension/force changes in the muscle throughout the range of
motion"* — a cable preacher curl is easy at long muscle lengths and hard at short ones. A growing body
of work suggests training at **longer muscle lengths** confers advantages for hypertrophy.
· [Stronger by Science — range of motion and muscle length](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/rom/)

⚠️ **THIS IS THE WEAKEST-SOURCED OF THE FOUR AXES for substitution purposes.** The lengthened-position
literature is about how to CHOOSE an exercise, not about how close two exercises are. Treat profile as
a reason to prefer, never as a reason to reject a same-muscle same-pattern swap.

### 1d. Variation itself has a cost and a benefit

A 2022 systematic review found **some systematic variation enhances regional hypertrophy and dynamic
strength, while excessive random variation compromises gains.** A separate trial found varying
exercise selection improved training motivation with similar muscular adaptations.
· [Does varying resistance exercises promote superior hypertrophy? (JSCR 2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/)
· [Exercise variation, muscle thickness and motivation (PMC6934277)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6934277/)

**What this licenses:** rotating between a cell's offered options across a block. **What it forbids:**
treating a long, unranked option list as harmless — random variation is the failure mode named.

### 1e. THE LIKE-FOR-LIKE TEST — OURS, derived from 1a–1d

A substitution is like-for-like when it holds, in this order:

1. **Same prime mover.** Non-negotiable; the cell exists to train a muscle.
2. **Same movement pattern / plane.** The frame states it, and 1a's specificity caveat is about
   pattern as much as implement.
3. **Loadable to the cell's intent.** A volume cell needs progressive load (see §3).
4. **Similar stability demand, or the difference named.** From 1b — a swap that changes it is
   legitimate and must be visible.
5. **Resistance profile — a preference, not a gate.** From 1c.

## §2. Ranking a cell's options for one athlete

### 2a. Stimulus-to-fatigue is the field's own ranking heuristic

Coined by Mike Israetel (Renaissance Periodization): the best exercises **stimulate the most growth
for the least fatigue**, and low-SFR movements are the ones to swap out.
· [RP — complete hypertrophy training guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/complete-hypertrophy-training-guide)
· [Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio explained](https://outlift.com/stimulus-to-fatigue-ratio-sfr/)
· [Juggernaut — stimulus-fatigue relationship](https://www.jtsstrength.com/stimulus-fatigue-relationship/)

⚠️ **SFR IS A HEURISTIC, NOT A MEASURED QUANTITY.** No source gives a formula or a per-exercise table.
It justifies HAVING a ranking; it does not supply one. Anything claiming a numeric SFR is invention.

### 2b. What the field does NOT settle

· **No published ranking of substitutes.** No source found ranks candidate substitutes for a given
  cell. Named searches: exercise substitution ranking, alternative exercise algorithm, exercise
  selection criteria across NSCA, SBS, RP and JTS.
· **No evidence on option-list length.** The dropdown-length question is a usability question, and no
  training-science source speaks to it.
· **Free weight vs machine is settled as "no difference"** (1a) — so a ranking that puts free weights
  above machines on principle is **not** supported. Rank on the athlete's kit, not on modality.

## §3. Loadability and the intent of the cell

A hypertrophy cell is volume under load. The exercise-variation and SFR literature both assume a
loadable, progressible movement; neither addresses bodyweight-only movements offered in a volume slot.

**OURS, and stated as ours:** a hypertrophy cell excludes bodyweight-only movements **for an athlete
who owns something to load with**. A bodyweight athlete's cell is never emptied, because for them the
catalogue is bodyweight and the alternative is no work at all.

⚠️ Viada's own strength dose (p80) is *4–6 reps above 90%* plus *15–20 velocity reps at 70–85%* — both
expressed as percentages of a maximum, which presumes a loadable movement. That is the source's
implicit agreement, not an explicit statement about bodyweight work.

## §4. What the commercial apps do — SECONDARY SOURCES ONLY

⚠️ **READ THE PROVENANCE.** These are review sites describing app behaviour, not the apps' own
documentation, and none discloses an algorithm. Cite them for *"this is field-standard practice"* and
never for a mechanism.

· **Fitbod** offers a one-tap substitution to *"an alternative exercise that targets the same muscle
  groups"*, and its generator selects on **available equipment** plus muscle fatigue and training
  history.
· **Dr. Muscle** likewise selects exercises on equipment availability.
· **Hevy, Strong, JEFIT** do not generate algorithmically — they are loggers, and substitution is the
  athlete's own.
· [Hevy vs Strong vs Fitbod](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/hevy-vs-strong-vs-fitbod)
· [Fitbod alternatives compared](https://www.boostcamp.app/alternatives/fitbod)
· [Hevy Coach — exercise selection glossary](https://hevycoach.com/glossary/exercise-selection/)

**What the field standard actually is, on this evidence:** *same muscle group + athlete's equipment*.
That is exactly §1's first and third tests, and it is thinner than what this app already does — no
source shows an app matching on movement pattern or stability.

## §5. THE ORDERING RULE — OURS, derived above

For a cell's option list, in order:

1. **The source's own printed movements for this cell**, in the order the page prints them. Nothing
   in §1–§4 outranks the programme being followed; the substitution question only arises where the
   kit reaches none of them.
2. **Same prime mover, same pattern, closest stability** — the like-for-like test of §1e, most tests
   held first.
3. **Same prime mover, pattern held, stability changed** — legitimate (§1a) and marked.
4. **Everything else, last**, and only where the cell would otherwise be empty.

**Excluded outright, not ranked last:**
· Movements that fail the cell's intent — bodyweight in a volume cell for a loaded athlete (§3).
· Duplicates of a movement already offered under another name (see the app's own load-variant rule).
· Movements the athlete's kit cannot reach at all.

⚠️ **AND THE RANKING IS NOT MODALITY-BASED.** §1a rules that out explicitly: at a commercial gym the
machine version is not a downgrade, and at a home kit the free-weight version is not a compromise.
What ranks is how many of §1e's tests the option holds **for this athlete's kit**.

⛔ **WHAT THIS DOC CANNOT TELL YOU.** How many options a row should show. Whether a specific ranking
reads well on a phone. Both are screen judgements with no evidence behind them, and this file says so
rather than inventing a number.
