# STRENGTH (pp.218–223)

## Repetition/set guidelines (p218)

| Intent | Reps | % | Effort | Sets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ME** | 1–5 | 90–100% | no RIR target | 1–3 |
| **DE** | 2–4 | 70–80% | maximum velocity (3–4 RIR) | 4–6 |
| **SKILL** | 3–5 | 75–85% | controlled eccentric, fast concentric (3–4 RIR) | 3–5 |
| **HYP** | 6–12 | *(none printed)* | controlled eccentric, controlled concentric (0–2 RIR) | 3–4 |

**Sets (p218):** *"Sets should always remain on the lower end when starting a program, increasing only
if an athlete is finding that they are progressing well and seem to have recovery to spare!"*

**What the intents mean (p219):**
- **ME** — moving maximal or near-maximal weight; peak force per rep; bar speed secondary. Each set
  stopped short of failure — technical breakdown here is counterproductive.
- **DE** — bar speed and movement quality; treat every rep as though the bar were maximal. Fatigue
  discouraged.
- **SKILL** — patterning and movement practice. Heavy enough to challenge, but form and consistency
  over velocity.
- **HYP** — bodybuilding-style, maximum motor unit recruitment, steady tempo. Fatigue is *not* the
  enemy; reps slowing as fast-twitch fibres tire is desirable.
- **RIR** — reps in reserve before failure. 2 RIR ends with two left. **0 RIR is not failure** — the
  final rep still completes, very slowly.

## PRIMARY (p218–219)

> Compound movements, barbell or bar, cardinal plane of movement (vertical/horizontal) or contest-/
> assessment-specific movement with or without minor modifications to setup (push/pull/hinge/lower push)

- **Primary push upper** — Bench press · Military press · Push press
- **Primary pull** — Pull-up · Barbell row
- **Primary hinge** — Deadlift · Paused deadlift · Sumo deadlift · Trap bar deadlift
- **Primary push lower** — Back squat · Front squat · Box squats

## SECONDARY (p220)

> Compound noncontested movements, dumbbell variants (variable form, plane of movement,
> push/pull/hinge/lower push)

- **Secondary push upper** — Larsen press · Incline bench press · Close-grip bench press · JM press ·
  Seated DB press · Arnold press
- **Secondary pull upper** — Kroc row · T-bar row · Meadows row · Gorilla row · DB pullovers
- **Secondary hinge lower** — Romanian deadlift · Stiff-legged deadlift · Bench reverse hyper ·
  Good morning · KB swing · Sandbag throw
- **Secondary press lower** — Split squat · Zercher squat · Freestanding barbell calf raises ·
  Forward or reverse lunge

## BRACED (p221–222)

> More externally braced movements (push/pull/hinge/lower push)

- **Braced push upper** — Smith machine press · Machine chest press · Dip machine/pressdown
- **Braced pull upper** — Chest-supported row (high row or low row) · Lat pulldowns (single or double,
  any grip) · Cable upright row
- **Braced push lower** — Hack squat · Leg press · Lever squat
- **Braced hinge lower** — Reverse hyperextension (machine) · GHD back extension · Ground-based
  deadlift machine · Machine back extension

## FOCUSED (p222–223)

> Single-joint emphasis (chest/deltoid/lat/hamstring/quad/calf/biceps/triceps)

- **Focused push/arms** — Triceps pushdowns · Tate press · Behind-the-neck DB triceps extensions ·
  Skull crushers · Pec deck · Lateral raises
- **Focused pull/arms** — Preacher curls · Spider curls · Rear delt machine · Drag curls ·
  Pullover machine
- **Focused push lower/quads** — Leg extensions · Hip adduction machine · Weighted knee raises
  (hip flexors) · Seated calf raises
- **Focused hinge lower/hamstrings** — Machine/Smith machine hip thrust · Hamstring curls (seated or
  prone) · Cable or machine kickbacks

## CORE EXERCISES (p223)

Hanging leg raises · Crunches · V-ups · Dynamic plank variants · Ab wheel rollouts

# OLYMPIC LIFTS — designation OLY (pp.224–225)

Its own intent vocabulary, separate from ME/DE/SKILL/HYP.

| Intent | Loading |
|---|---|
| **HEAVY** | 90% plus, singles |
| **REP** | 80–90%, steady repetitions until form compromised |
| **SKILL** | 75–85%, singles, until form target reached |
| **GROOVE** | 70–80%, quality singles through triples, nonfatiguing, stopping well shy of breakdown |

- **PRIMARY ("PRIMARY")** — *any full lift, performed to competition standard* — Full clean ·
  Jerk (split or push) · Full clean and jerk · Full snatch
- **PARTIAL VARIANT ("VARIANT")** — *any component or partial range of motion variant of a competition
  lift* — Clean from low/high hang · Clean low pulls · Hang snatch · Split jerk/push jerk off blocks ·
  Clean/snatch from blocks
- **MODIFIED VARIANT ("MOD")** — *any lift performed with altered movement patterns/patterns not seen
  in a competition lift* — Tall jerk · Push jerk (competition legal, but rarely performed) ·
  Clean high pulls · Drop snatch · Snatch balance · Power variants of competition lifts

**Additional abbreviations (p225):**
- **HEAVY** — high-percentage sets requiring mental and physical preparation for a high-skill movement
  at high output. If mis-grooving early in a session, avoid heavy sets and do skill or groove instead —
  poor practice with heavy loads is disproportionately counterproductive.
- **REP** — "clustered singles": moving solid, challenging weight multiple times with less setup than
  singles. Exposes balance and bar-path issues quickly.
- **GROOVE** — same loading as skill, different objective. Skill work *builds* toward proper form;
  groove work is quality practice once already warm and moving well. Moving poorly on heavy or rep
  work → do skill sets until form returns, then drop slightly for groove until fatigue sets in.

# CARRY / DRAG / PICK (p226)

> Movements where the weight is transported from one place to another. A "pick" is simply picking up
> the implement and putting it down without forward movement. Several movements may also qualify as
> hinge/pull/press movements during the "pick" phase, most notably farmer's/frame carries and tire flips.

- **Axial loading / carry variants** — Farmer's carry · Yoke walk · Frame carry · Sandbag carry ·
  Zercher carry · Duck walk
- **Push/pull variants** — Sled push · Sled pull · Truck push · Plate push · Sandbag drag
- **Dynamic movement variants** — Tire flip · Fingal's Fingers

**Its own intent guidelines (p226):**
- **ME** — near maximal weight, initial "pick/pickup" is a challenge, several steps of movement is 9/10 RPE
- **DE** — light weight, emphasis is speed and turnover in movement, fatigue accumulation from rapid
  "reps" is expected
- **SKILL** — medium weight, emphasis is speed and quality, no fatigue accumulation, ample rest
- **HYP** — medium weight, steady velocity, fatigue accumulation is target stimulus

# PLYOMETRIC DRILLS — PLYO (p227)

- **Bounding and skipping/dynamic movement** — A/B skips · Distance bounding · Prime times
  (stiff-legged run)
- **Control and ground contact time reduction** — Single-leg hops · Rebound jumps · Skater hops ·
  Lunge hops · Pogo hops
- **Footspeed and movement drills** — Ladder drills (footspeed and mobility, not true plyometrics) ·
  Ickey Shuffle · Hopscotch

**How they are run (p227):** all done separately. ⛔ *"Throwing more than three or four plyometric
movements together on a given day is likely a waste of time."* Each drill performed multiple times
with ample rest, full focus on technique, balance and consistent quality; done until the movement is
optimised for the day and the athlete has confidence in it, then move on. **Fatigue, poor form and
imprecise movements are absolute no-nos.**

⚠️ He states the lists here *"barely even scratch the surface"* — they are examples of the buckets,
not a closed set. Bounding benefits running gait/speed; ground contact drills improve general speed
and explosiveness; footspeed drills are foot/leg control rather than true agility.
