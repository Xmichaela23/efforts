# DOCTRINE — the threshold run, and its 12-week map (2026-08-17)

**Michael, 2026-08-17.** The prescribed sustained run inside a Strong Focus block: what it is, how it
is paced, what ground it happens on, and how it changes across twelve weeks.

**One sentence:** twenty minutes of strict lactate-threshold work at 5K + 20 s/mi, on flat or rolling
ground where effort outranks pace, progressed by lengthening the effort and then by quickening it —
and then handed back to the barbell for the anchor.

---

## 0. The rule this session exists to obey — no junk volume

If the athlete is lifting heavy barbells, endurance work may only live at the extremes: **easy
aerobic**, or **strictly capped top-end work**. The middle is what costs the block.

**The grey zone — tempo running that is neither easy nor threshold — builds systemic fatigue without
triggering a specific high-end adaptation, and it spends the recovery the heavy lifts need.** That is
the reason this session is defined tightly rather than described loosely: an under-specified "tempo
run" drifts into the grey zone by default.

## 1. The session

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pace** | **5K pace + 20–25 s/mi.** |
| **Why that number** | 5K pace is VO2 max work — lactate accumulates and the athlete is failing toward the finish. Stepping down ~20 s/mi lands on the **lactate threshold**: the tipping point where clearance matches production. Above it the session becomes a short VO2 session; below it, grey-zone volume. |
| **Terrain** | **Flat or rolling uninterrupted path.** Track, flat road or path, or treadmill at **1% incline**. |
| **Why** | Power output must stay static to train clearance. A climb run at flat-ground pace spikes heart rate into the VO2 band, floods the legs, and the threshold adaptation is destroyed. |
| **Rest** | **2 minutes** easy jog or walk between efforts, every week of the block. Enough for heart rate to fall slightly, then straight back onto pace. |
| **Warm-up / cool-down** | 10 minutes easy jog either side. |
| **Working time** | **20–21 minutes in the leader waves; deliberately LESS in the anchor** — see §2. |
| **Whole session** | ~40–42 minutes in the leader waves. |

⛔ **THE CAP IS THE POINT, NOT A BUDGET.** This session lands on the same day as a heavy squat or
deadlift. Twenty minutes of redline is enough to pull the aerobic ceiling up and short enough that
the legs and the nervous system survive the barbell. **A progression that grows total working time
past ~30 minutes defeats the whole arrangement.** Progress the session by making the twenty minutes
harder, never longer.

## 1b. ⛔ EFFORT OUTRANKS PACE — the rule that survives real roads

**Michael, 2026-08-17.** A perfectly flat route is a laboratory condition. Outside a track, an
athlete will meet 2–3% rolling grades, and the target pace stops being achievable at threshold
effort.

⚠️ **GEOGRAPHICALLY AGNOSTIC ON PURPOSE.** No city, no named road, no terrain type beyond the angle
of the ground. The rule keys on the GRADE in front of the athlete and their own biological feedback,
so it reads the same everywhere on earth. ⛔ Do not "make it concrete" with a local example.

**The rule: maintain the EFFORT, not the pace.**

> On a 2–3% rise, consciously slow by **10–15 s/mi** to hold heart rate at threshold. On the flat,
> accelerate back to target.

⛔ **AND THE FAILURE MODE IS PSYCHOLOGICAL, NOT PHYSIOLOGICAL — which is why it has to be said out
loud.** Hand an athlete `7:50/mi` and a hill, and ego makes them hold the number to keep the watch
happy. They cross the redline, flood the legs, and **blame the barbell programme when the squat
fails the following week.** The number is a proxy; the effort is the prescription. **The athlete
needs explicit permission to miss the number when the geography demands it**, or they will not take
it.

### Where this is said — three surfaces, front-end copy only
⚠️ **NO NEW STORED FIELDS.** The athlete is given a target number and the permission to fail it.
That is presentation, not data.

| surface | what it carries |
|---|---|
| **The terrain menu**, when they pick the environment | Under *Flat road or path*: *"If you hit mild inclines or rolling grades (2–3%), yield the pace to maintain the effort. Do not spike your heart rate on uphills."* |
| **The daily session card**, on the day | A target AND a cap, not a rigid pace. **Target:** the pace, marked *flat ground*. **Effort cap:** threshold — lactate equilibrium. **Note:** *"Pace is a proxy for effort. If the road tilts up, consciously slow by 10–15 seconds per mile. If you are gasping or redlining, you are stealing recovery from your heavy lifting. Yield to the hill."* |
| **The block intro**, once | *"Threshold work sits right below your redline. If you force your flat-ground pace up a hill, you cross the redline, flood your legs with lactic acid, and destroy the recovery capital your nervous system needs for heavy squats. Let the GPS watch look slow so the barbell can stay heavy."* |

⚠️ **COPY-VOICE:** the session note and the block intro are fact-then-consequence, which is the house
style. The one imperative — *"Yield to the hill"* — is Michael's own wording and is kept.

## 2. The 12-week map

It mirrors the barbell's 3:1 rhythm exactly — three working weeks, then the light week takes the
intervals off entirely.

### Wave 1 — Leader 1: establish the baseline
**Lever: the length of the continuous effort.** Pace fixed at 5K + 20 s. Rest 2 min.

| week | session |
|---|---|
| 1 | 4 × 5 min |
| 2 | 3 × 7 min |
| 3 | 2 × 10 min |

### Week 4 — light week
⛔ **NO INTERVALS.** The hard session is deleted from the calendar. An easy conversational aerobic
run replaces it. *You cannot adapt if you never clear the fatigue.*

### Wave 2 — Leader 2: advance the wave
**Lever: pace.** The identical structure to wave 1, run **5–10 s/mi faster**. Rest stays 2 min.

| week | session |
|---|---|
| 5 | 4 × 5 min — faster target |
| 6 | 3 × 7 min — faster target |
| 7 | 2 × 10 min — faster target |

### Week 8 — light week
⛔ **NO INTERVALS.** Easy conversational run only.

### Wave 3 — Anchor: the RUN YIELDS
⛔ **CORRECTED 2026-08-17, AND THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS SECTION BROKE THE SYSTEM'S OWN LAW.** It read
*"6 × 3 min near 5K race pace, 90 s rest"* — mirroring the AMRAP. That is a VO2 stressor, and it was
scheduled on the same day as a Wendler AMRAP set taken toward failure. **Two peak nervous-system
efforts on one day is the collapse this whole arrangement exists to prevent.**

**In the anchor the barbell wins and the endurance yields.** The run drops to maintenance: a
high-quality flush that holds the adaptation the leader waves bought, at a fraction of the time
under tension, so the nervous system arrives at the AMRAP loaded.

- **Pace:** back to wave 1 — **5K + 20 s. Do not run faster.** ⛔ No 5K-pace work in this block.
- **Rest:** back to **2 minutes**.
- **Volume:** slashed.

| week | session | working time |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | 3 × 5 min | 15 min |
| 10 | 2 × 7 min | 14 min |
| 11 | 1 × 10 min | 10 min |

### Week 12 — TM test week
⛔ **NO INTERVALS.** The barbell maximums are being measured; the hard run comes off. Easy aerobic
flush only.

### The shape of it
Working time peaks at **20–21 minutes** in the leader waves and **falls away through the anchor**
(15 → 14 → 10). The block builds the aerobic ceiling across weeks 1–7 by lengthening the effort and
then quickening it, and then **spends nothing defending it** while the barbell expresses peak
strength. The 30-minute ceiling is never approached in any week.

⚠️ **SO THE RUN'S PEAK AND THE BARBELL'S PEAK ARE DELIBERATELY OUT OF PHASE.** The hardest running
in the block is week 7, not week 11. That is the arrangement, not an oversight — if a later change
makes the anchor's run harder "for symmetry with the bar", it is reintroducing the double-peak this
section was rewritten to remove.
