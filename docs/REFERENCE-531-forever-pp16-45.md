# REFERENCE — 5/3/1 Forever, pp.16–45 · read 2026-08-15

**What this is.** The load-bearing facts from *5/3/1 Forever* (Wendler, 2017), pages 16–45,
read from page photos of Michael's physical copy in the 2026-08-15 session. Facts, numbers and
structure only, pinned to pages, with short quotes where exact wording is load-bearing.

⛔ **The book itself is NOT in this repo and must not be committed** — same rule as the 2011
PDF. This doc exists so sessions can verify claims without the book.

⚠️ **Scope: pp.16–45 ONLY.** The book is ~600 pages of templates. Anything attributed to Forever
outside these pages is secondary until someone reads it. In particular: the named templates
(Krypteia, BBS, SSL details, 5x5/3/1, Widowmaker, leader/anchor pairings per template) are NOT
covered here.

⚠️ **pp.38–44 are "Beginner Prep School"** — a specific template for novices. Its numbers (3-week
waves at 70/80/90, A/B full-body days, assistance circuits) belong to that template, not to
5/3/1 generally. Marked [BPS] below. Do not cite [BPS] rows as general rules.

## Supplemental lifts (p.15–17)

- Supplemental = barbell lifts that "closely resemble the main lifts" (p.15). Named schemes:
  Boring But Big, First Set Last, Second Set Last, Boring But Strong (p.15).
- Supplemental can be done with the main lift itself or an alternate lift; an alternate needs
  its own tested TM (p.15).
- Supplemental option lists (p.16): deadlift → deficit DL, rack pull, pull from blocks, snatch
  grip DL, fat bar DL, double overhand DL, trap bar DL. Squat → front squat, box squat, safety
  bar, Manta Ray. Bench/press → grip variants, incline, floor press, football-bar and log-bar
  variants.
- **Not every 5/3/1 program has a supplemental lift** (p.17). Optional by template.
- p.17, on weak points: until a lifter has basic strength and fitness (10 chin-ups, 20 hanging
  leg raises, a mile run), specialty supplemental variation is noise. "People's weak points are
  rarely muscles."

## Programming models (p.17)

- Plans are programmed 3, 4 or 5 cycles ahead; newer lifters plan longer, advanced shorter.
- Three models: **3 Leaders/2 Anchors** (beginners + BBB/BBS challenges; "I do not recommend
  this for most people"), **2 Leaders/2 Anchors** (beginner, intermediate), **2 Leaders/1
  Anchor** (beginner→advanced; recommended "for just about every lifter and almost every
  program").
- The 2/1 rationale: enough time to make and evaluate progress, a mini-peak, and a check that
  the TM is right. Spending more than two cycles on the very hard programs "will burn you out."

## Leader vs Anchor (p.18–19)

- **Leader template** (p.18): increase in barbell volume, usually via supplemental. LESS
  assistance volume. LESS jumps/throws. LESS hard conditioning. Emphasis on easy conditioning.
- **Anchor template** (p.18): decrease in barbell volume, higher intensity. MORE assistance.
  MORE jumps/throws. MORE hard conditioning. Easy conditioning still done.
- Mobility stays constant across templates (p.18 note).
- Anchors: "the barbell volume will be lower and you will still do easy conditioning" (p.19).
- Most programs in the book come as a Leader and an Anchor version; pick honestly against your
  goals and capacity (p.19).

## The 7th Week Protocol (p.19–23)

- Name is a label, not a calendar rule: "not done every seventh week; it's just a name" (p.19).
- Three functions: deload · TM test · PR test (p.19). In all three: reduced volume, **no
  supplemental**, limited assistance (p.19).
- General rules (p.19): used as the deload **between Leader and Anchor**; used to test TMs at
  the completion of both Leader and Anchor; PR version rare, program-specific; may be used as a
  deload after ANY cycle, especially older lifters and taxing programs — "this is your
  responsibility."
- **TM guidance** (p.20): original program TM = 90% of actual/estimated 1RM. With the added
  supplemental volume, "beginning with an 85% training max is often recommended." Everyone in
  his weight room starts at 80–85%; he stays 85–90% for most; stronger lifters lower, some at
  77%. "The more progress you can make with a lighter training max the better off you will be."
  After a year+, expect different TMs per lift (p.20).
- **7th Week, TM test** (p.20): 70%×5, 80%×5, 90%×5, 100%(TM) × 3–5. Pass bar: ≥3 reps for a
  90% TM, **5 reps for an 85% TM**. Reps should be "strong and fast," never a true 3RM/5RM.
- **Even a big test set does not raise the increment** (p.20, bold in book): more than five
  reps → "stay the course. Do not increase more than the normal amount each cycle." Repeated in
  the PRs Q&A (p.20–21): 8 reps at 95%, should I jump more than 5/10 lb? "The answer every
  single time is NO" — bar speed suffers, plateau, burnout.
- Krypteia anecdote (p.21): 40 kids at 85% TM; after three cycles half could do 15+ reps at TM;
  the fix is still only the basic 5–10 lb inching forward.
- **TM too high** (p.21): only 1–2 reps at TM → lower it. Recompute estimated max
  (weight × reps × .0333 + weight) and set TM to 85–90% of that. Many programs require an 85%
  TM, a few 80%. Personally: fewer than "five strong reps" at TM → he lowers it. "I have seen
  way too much success with about an 85% TM for it to be a coincidence."
- **Bold rule** (p.21): "Prior to any Leader template, I recommend you perform a training max
  test week."
- **7th Week, deload** (p.21): 70%×5, 80%×3–5, 90%×1, 100%(TM)×1. For those who need a break
  and know the TM is correct. "The reason to deload is so that you never have to deload."
  Bold: "This deload is always used between your Leader and Anchor template." May be used after
  any cycle if needed.
- **7th Week, PR test** (p.21): 70%×5, 80%×5, 90%×5, 100%(TM) × PR/goal. Good for 5's-PRO-heavy
  training and athletes who train light but want a competitive week. Miss 3–5 reps on it → adjust
  the TM.
- Scheduling variants (p.22): 4-day (one lift per day Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri), 3-day (squat / bench /
  **deadlift + press share Friday**), 2-day (squat+bench / deadlift+press). Every day: warm-up,
  jumps/throws 10 total, lift(s), assistance.
- 7th-week assistance (p.23): 25–50 total reps per category per workout; may decrease further
  or switch to less intensive movements (dips/DB bench → pushdowns example).
- 7th-week conditioning (p.23): 3–5 easy days; conditioning tests allowed (mile, Prowler goal);
  avoid hard conditioning otherwise; don't squat hard the day before a running test.
- **Reset expectation** (p.23, bold-italic in book): "You will have to reset your training
  maxes frequently." Especially press/bench. Not every lift moves at the same rate. Many
  programs may need a deload after every cycle.
- Sequencing summary (p.23, bold): between every Leader and Anchor → 7th Week Deload. Prior to
  new programming → 7th Week TM Test or PR Test.

## Assistance work (p.24–34)

- Three categories per training day: **push, pull, single leg/core** (p.24).
- **Base 5/3/1 recommendation: 50–100 total reps per category per workout** (p.24). Each
  template carries its own specific numbers; "you can choose to do more or less."
- One or two exercises per category per workout (p.32). Mix and match freely; changing
  exercises workout to workout is fine — "It is the work that matters" (p.24).
- Balance rule (p.24): with heavy squat/DL volume, cut lower-back assistance and heavy rows;
  use face pulls, band pull-aparts, easier ab work. "Not everything should be 'in the red.'"
- Category rosters (pp.24–31): PUSH — dips (higher reps, 8+; not every workout, once/twice a
  week; 100-rep example = 50 weighted + 50 bodyweight), push-ups (weighted via vest/chains;
  tightness teacher for bench), DB presses (full ROM), triceps ext/pushdowns, plate raise.
  PULL — chin-ups/pull-ups (vary grips; ropes/towels for grip; low reps OK), inverted "fat man"
  rows, rows (DB recommended over barbell for lower-back stress; DB row grip work 20–50 reps
  heavy), curls (balance vs pressing, shoulder-injury prevention), lat pulldown (fine; chins
  still preferred), face pulls (lots of reps, never maxed), band pull-aparts ("Most Boring but
  Effective"), rear laterals, upright row, shrugs. SINGLE LEG/CORE — back raise / reverse hyper
  (high-rep lower back; strengthens squat and pull), glute ham raise, SLDL/GM (assistance form,
  light — "not a powerlifting contest"), abdominal work (ab wheel + hanging leg raise his two
  picks; higher reps/slower sets; javelin throwers' 50 straight-leg raises), single-leg
  movements (step-ups, lunges, Bulgarians — form over load; short steps turn it into a two-leg
  movement), KB swing/clean/snatch (assistance tool, not essential; barbell "is still the
  king"), farmer walk (back/grip/core strength, NOT conditioning for beginners; 240–400 total
  yards typical), goblet squat (beginners + Krypteia), neck (optional; 50–100 total reps).
- Common-sense pairing (p.32): harder templates → easier-to-recover assistance (bodyweight,
  face pulls, pull-aparts). Easier templates / less supplemental → more challenging assistance.
- **Bodyweight capacity standards** (p.33), 10-minute tests, max reps in 10:00, standards for
  men: push-ups 100 · dips 75 · chin/pull-ups 50 · hanging leg raises 50. Test one movement per
  workout, every couple of weeks, not the day before that muscle's heavy work. Weighted-vest
  versions at 10% and 20% bodyweight for those past the standards (p.33–34); vest goal = same
  numbers at +20% BW (p.34).
- Weaker lifters: bodyweight assistance should not be the ONLY choice — still use DBs, KBs,
  face pulls (p.33).

## Beginner Prep School [BPS] (pp.38–44) — template-specific, for the future beginner plan

- Purpose: "build a foundation" — a Classical Training Education. Training = jumping, lifting,
  running, calisthenics (p.38). Three lifting days/week, A/B alternating full-body workouts.
- Calisthenics warm-up circuit ×3: jumping jacks 25, BW squats 10, mountain climbers 10/leg
  (p.38). Then jumps: box jump or standing long jump, ~10–20 total per workout; "depth jumps
  are not advised" (p.38–39).
- TM setup (p.39): careful 5-rep-set work-ups with 5–10% jumps; stop on bar-speed loss, no
  misses needed. TM = 85–90% of estimated max (Epley). 90% for stronger/experienced lifts,
  **85% for lifts they struggle with** — lighter while form corrects.
- Workouts (p.39–40): A = squat + bench, B = deadlift + press. Both lifts 5/3/1-style with 5s:
  W1 70/80/90×5, W2 65/75/85×5, W3 75/85/95×5, each + **5×5 FSL** at the first-set percentage.
  "The varying percentage sets... provide the main work for the lift, and the following sets
  provide the supplemental work (in this case using First Set Last for 5 sets of 5 reps)"
  (p.40). Weaker lifts (85% TM) use Second Set Last instead, same 5×5 (p.40).
- Between-sets filler (p.40): push-ups between squat work; sit-ups/chins/inverted rows/face
  pulls/pull-aparts during supplemental. "None of this is to failure."
- Time goals (p.41, p.44): each lift + its supplemental in 15–18 min; both lifts 30–36 min;
  assistance in 20 min. Don't move to another template until the time goal is met naturally.
- Assistance [BPS] (p.41–42): circuit style, 4 exercises, 3–5 sets: KB swing/snatch or DB/BW
  squat 25–100 total · push-ups/dips 25–100 · chin-ups/pull-ups 25–50 · ab wheel/hanging leg
  raise 25–50. 20-minute goal. Can't do chins → inverted rows. Single-leg work 5–10 reps/set,
  competence-gated.
- Running [BPS] (p.43): 3×/week, goal = run one mile; track options 100m×10–16 · 200m×6–8 ·
  400m×4–6 · 800m×2–3, run not sprint. Off-day extra work = bodyweight assistance, allowed
  daily.
- Progression [BPS] (p.44): milk the program; after each cycle raise TMs, possibly only +5 on
  squat/DL for weak kids, or repeat a cycle, or use fractional plates. No timetable.
- **Stall menu [BPS]** (p.44) — all begin "Back up three cycles and…": repeat · push last set
  for PR/goal (only with sound technique) · increase supplemental volume (7–10×5 FSL) · perform
  5×5/3/1 · use SSL. (Referenced against the main book for the latter two.)
- Five goals (p.44): both lifts in 30–36 min; assistance in 20; run a mile; jump/land
  correctly; groundwork for balanced training.

## Boring But Big (p.45)

- Always a Leader template. For gaining muscle; strength gains in younger lifters.
- **"Not a good option for athletes, true beginners and very advanced lifters."**
- 85% TM for most lifters, 90% for beginners.
- Beginners fail multiple sets of ten (fatigue breaks form); beginner test: "If you are asking
  the question... then you are one," or <1 year of consistent correct training. Advanced
  lifters keep BBB percentages very low (60–70% of a 300 lb squat vs 550 lb squat argument).
- Plateau advice above the BBB header (p.45): moving to another template is a legitimate answer
  to a plateau.
